# Safety

**Effective date:** [EFFECTIVE_DATE]

Opora is here to listen and to help you reflect. It is **not a therapist, not a doctor, and not a crisis service**. This page explains what that means and where to turn when you need more than a journal.

---

## If you are in danger right now

If you are thinking about ending your life, about harming yourself or someone else, or you are in immediate danger:

- **Call your local emergency number**: **112** across the European Union and in Georgia, **911** in the United States and Canada, **999** in the United Kingdom.
- **Contact a crisis line.** They are free, confidential, and staffed by people trained to help:
  - **United States:** call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline)
  - **United Kingdom and Ireland:** call **116 123** (Samaritans)
  - **Anywhere else:** find a helpline in your country at **[findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com)**
- If you can, **tell someone you trust** — a friend, a family member, a neighbour — and do not stay alone.

Reaching out is not weakness. It is taking care of yourself.

## What Opora can and cannot do

**Opora can:**

- give you a space to put your thoughts and feelings into words;
- respond with support, and reflect back what it notices;
- help you see patterns in your days over time.

**Opora cannot:**

- diagnose or treat any condition, or replace a psychologist, psychotherapist, psychiatrist or doctor;
- see or hear you — it knows only what you write;
- monitor your conversations in real time, notice that you have gone quiet, or check on you;
- call anyone, contact emergency services, or act on your behalf in any way.

Opora's replies are generated by AI. They can be wrong, and they can miss things that a person would notice. If something Opora says feels off, trust yourself, not the app.

---

## When to talk to a professional

Consider reaching out to a qualified professional if, for example:

- low mood, anxiety or numbness has lasted for weeks and is affecting your sleep, appetite, work or relationships;
- you are using alcohol, drugs, food or other behaviours to cope and it is getting out of hand;
- you have experienced something traumatic and it keeps coming back;
- you are not safe in your relationship or at home;
- you notice thoughts of not wanting to be here.

Your doctor or general practitioner is often a good first step and can refer you to the right specialist.
